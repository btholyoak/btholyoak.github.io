1. 11/22/63 - Stephen King This is another great book from King. I really enjoyed the premise and the "what if" aspect of the book. The ending was pretty decent for a King book, I've had some complaints in the past about how King ends some of his stories, but this was one of his better ones. There are some parts in the book that kind of bogged down for me but overall the pacing was pretty good.
  2. Resurrection Walk (Lincoln Lawyer) - Michael Connelly Another book in the Lincoln Lawyers series, I wasn't too impressed with this story since it seemed there wasn't much twists and turns as some of the previous books. The back and forth with the judge and ending have been thoroughly fleshed out in some of the other books. With all that said it's still not a bad book but may not be the most interesting if you have read the other books in the series.
  3. How to Build a Car - Adrian Newey I liked the start of the book as it was interesting to see how his passion for designing cars started at a relatively young age. However, I wasn't really hooked when he started getting into the middle of his career and I found myself not wanting to read, so I never finished it.
  4. The Wager - David Grann I loved all of the little tidbits of information this book gives you regarding sailing in the 1700s. It's miraculous what they were able to accomplish with their understanding and technology back then. The author does a good job of creating an interesting story out of old journal records. Additionally it was cool to see a glimpse into the politics and culture around Britain's navy.
  5. Rita Hayworth and Shawshank Redemption - Stephen King Great story telling from King. King is great at making small side characters have great depth and interesting on their own. I liked how King doesn't give Red all the details of the story, and that you get to fill in a little on your own.
  6. Fahrenheit 451 - Ray Bradbury This book draws some scary parallels to what we are experiencing in society now a days. It brings up some interesting thoughts and dilemmas on what should the goal of a society should be for it's members (happiness?). I never read this book in high school but I understand why its a classic and probably should be read by most. Bradbury does have a "wax poetic" style which I'm usually not a fan of but he doesn't go overboard with it.
  7. The Scarecrow - Michael Connelly I'm not sure why I read these serial killer books they always make me depressed. This one is decent and has some suspenseful parts. However, my favorite part of these type of stories is the slow unveiling of the killer through clues and discoveries. This book doesn't do that, you learn who did it in either the first or second chapter. The author bakes suspense into the story because when he tells the story from the serial killer's perspective he gives away their intentions, so you know what is going to happen but not when. Not sure if I will read the third book in this series.
  8. The God of the Woods - Liz Moore I really enjoyed this book. The author does a great job of world building surrounding Camp Emerson and Self-Reliance. The interesting and conflicted characters really do a great job of making the story feel real. Good thriller! I might read more from Liz Moore I liked her style.
  9. The Shining - Stephen King I don't know how he does it but no other author can spook me like Stephen King can. There are parts of this book that remind me of when I was younger and I would turn off the light at the bottom of the stairs and sprint up the stairs to escape the darkness as if something was chasing me.
  10. Doctor Sleep - Stephen King The book wasn't bad by any means but it does fall a little flat compared to it's predecessor. Dan's struggles with addiction and recovery are the strongest parts of the book for me. The villains in this book are a bit boring and for being hundreds of years old seem pretty dumb. An interesting thing I noted while reading this is how much Stephen Kings writing style has changed over the years. It's not that its gotten worse it is just different.
  11. The Faithful Executioner - Joel F. Harrington This book was recommended to me by a coworker and while I do think it was a interesting book, I'm not sure I would be able to recommend it to most people. It was a pretty hard book to read due to the horrific nature of some of the crimes and their punishments. The executioner's story is one of perseverance but it is also a tragic one. A man shunned by a society due to his dishonorable profession yet he was more honorable than most.
  12. The Terror - Dan Simmons I thoroughly enjoyed this book, I'm not sure if it is as good as Hyperion so I might have to go back and read Hyperion again since it's been probably almost ten years at this point. I felt really attached with a lot of the more prominent characters in this book. I think my favorite character is Harry Goodsir a young naturalist who gave it his all given his circumstances and never faltered in his morality. I really enjoyed all of Goodsir's chapters even though I initially didn't like him when he seemed to have a holier than thou attitude. I also liked Irving even though I didn't find him as interesting as Goodsir, and Crozier is great. Good characters, good monster(s), and fuck Hickey the little rat.
  13. Cannery Row - John Steinbeck A charming little book about life and some of its oddities. A few times this book had me laughing out loud and tears welling up at other times. It's a fast, easy read and I would recommend it to anyone who likes a good story.
  14. Between Two Fires - Christopher Buehlman I love the idea of this book, God has left and no one knows where he went. The fallen angels take advantage of this and start to literally raise hell on earth. The story follows a knight, a priest, and a special little girl in a plague and devil ridden 1300's France as they try to make their way to Avignon. The story is primarily a series of encounters the party experiences on their journey. I liked this book, I enjoyed the characters and most of the encounters were interesting and if they weren't luckily most encounters were only a chapter at most. This is my biggest gripe with this book, the encounters are too short. I felt that there should have been less encounters and more time spent on the more interesting encounters. I thoroughly enjoyed the Penitents encounter and the scene afterwards is nothing short of incredible. I think this book could have placed the Penitents as the main opposition throughout the book and dived into more detail and been great. I think this style of book could have been better as a collection of short stories. I also think the story of Gabriel going out to find God would be a great story.
  15. The Spy And The Traitor - Ben Macintyre Interesting story about a KGB spy who was a double agent for MI6. I felt sad with the conclusion of this book.     1. All My Friends Are Going To Be Strangers - Larry McMurtry I have mixed feelings about this book.   18. The Stranger - Albert Camus, Matthew Ward (Translation) This is a strange story about a man who seems to have a rather unique outlook on life. While I wouldn't exactly describe it as nihilistic, it does share some key ideas, primarily the notion that nothing really matters. Nihilism often carries a negative connotation, but when you really break it down, it can actually have a rather positive outlook. Imagine if life had a singular, overarching point or purpose—there would be a kind of pressure to fulfill that purpose. Society's pressure to find meaning in life is already stressful enough; now imagine if there was a universal point you had to figure out. The idea that "there is no point" is a perfectly valid stance, and in fact, it can be quite freeing.

    As for my own philosophy, I wouldn't say there’s no point to life, but I do believe the point is simply to live it—whatever that means for you. I really enjoyed this book, and the final section with the priest was particularly compelling.

  20. Tell No One - Harlan Coben This book is bad. To start the author writes some very racist stereotyped characters. Particularly his portrayal of black and impoverished folks. It also felt like many plot points were loosely connected at best and really stretching the suspension of disbelief.   22. The Hunter - Richard Stark (Donald Westlake) I really enjoyed Westlake's writing style, gritty and detailed yet at other times beautiful and thought provoking. Solid story, I can see myself reading more of this series if I'm looking for a fast and fun read.
  23. Into Thin Air - Jon Krakauer Compelling story that is really well written. The author does a great job of building up the background for the story without ever bogging down the story's pace.
