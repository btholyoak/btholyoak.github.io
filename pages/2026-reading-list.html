1. The Devil In The White City - Erik Larson An interesting read about the construction of the world fair in Chicago in the 1890's and a serial killer who was active in Chicago around the time. The book is well written and the pacing is good considering some chapters are entirely devoted to rather dull topics at times. I found the chapters regarding the fair and it's construction to be more interesting than the Holmes chapters. I didn't like reading about Holmes much, I didn't find him or his activities to be particularly compelling; I did however enjoy the detective's perspective of the investigation that unveiled Holmes.
  2. Endurance - Alfred Lansing An expedition set out to traverse Antarctica on land ends up stranded afloat on ice in the Weddell sea. I am honestly amazed by the sheer resilience of these men to not only survive such an ordeal but to maintain a relatively positive attitude given the circumstances. The fact that they somehow managed to navigate 800 miles in a 22 foot open top boat through the Drake passage is nothing short of incredible. It really does live up to the name, Shackleton's Incredible Voyage.
  3. Say Nothing - Patrick Radden Keefe - 2/19/26 A mother of ten was taken and murder by the IRA during The Troubles in Northern Ireland. This book was an interesting look at the conflict between the United Kingdom and the republican paramilitaries of Northern Ireland. It provides first person accounts of members of the IRA, specifically the Provos wing. This wing committed extreme violence and numerous bombings throughout the conflict. It was troubling how people would justify their killings and the horrible things they did. I found myself saying "What is the point of all this suffering?" The callousness shown for human life on both sides of this conflict is appalling.
  4. Fairy Tale - Stephen King - 3/27/26 King has a wonderful ability to make even the mundane parts of his worlds interesting and fun. The first half of this book is literally about a boy helping out an old man and it's probably the best part of this book. I enjoyed reading this, albeit it took me longer than I would have liked. While not one of my favorites by King I think this could be a good book as a introduction into King possibly.
  5. Empire of Pain - Patrick Radden Keefe - 4/27/26 An insightful look into the Sackler family, Purdue Pharma, and the opioid epidemic. While reading this book I found myself getting increasingly frustrated with the government's failures to address this issue in any meaningful way and it's inability to hold any of the decision makers accountable. While I find the Sackler family absolutely deplorable and rotten to the core, I believe the biggest take away for me was how lobbying(corruption) seems to be a fundamental component to capitalism. Every attempt to bring justice was promptly halted by some shady back room deal. Very well written and researched.
  6. The Dispossessed - Ursula K. Le Guin - 5/22/26 Fantastic book, might be one of my all time favorites. The author hit the nail on the head for what a capitalist society inevitably arrives at, unfortunately it's very similar to modern society in the U.S. While the author clearly does not believe that capitalism is the answer she doesn't hide the issues that an anarchist society would face. I found her writing/ideas interesting and compelling. I would like to read more from her. Some quotes I liked: "If you evade suffering you also evade the chance of joy." "The thing about working with time, instead of against it, he thought, is that it is not wasted. Even pain counts."
  7. King Rat - James Clavell - 6/16/26 Terse but well written. A different perspective/experience on Japanese POW camps during WW2. I found the stories of the women's experiences to be interesting but their stories often felt incomplete. Which is fair I suppose since this book is about Changi but I would have liked to hear more of their stories. The book involves multiple rats in the POW camp and while it is up to the reader to decide who was the King Rat at the end of the day all of them were still rats in a cage.
  8. The Suitcase - Sergei Dovlatov - 6/30/26 Short book about the things from his 'past' life. Interesting book, it's nice to take a peak at a unique culture. "If I was only now feeling this for the first time, then how much love had been lost over the long years? I didn't have the strength to think it through. I never knew that love could be so strong and so sharp. I thought 'If my hands are shaking now, how will I feel in the future?' And so I got my coat and went to work."
  9. Stoner - John Williams - 7/14/26 It's a quite sad book, Stoner doesn't have many moments of pure happiness and they are fleeting at that. I remember someone describing Stoner as the "archetypal American man", I don't fully agree with that; I believe Stoner is the archetypal human experience. Stoner's experience was unremarkable, yet I imagine most could relate to him at one moment or another. The prose is crystal clear and sharp, the final chapter in this book is chock full of quotable insights. "He dimly recalled that he had been thinking of failure--as if it mattered. It seemed to him now that such thoughts were mean, unworthy of what his life had been."
  10. The Left Hand Of Darkness - Ursula K. Le Guin - 8/7/2026 Lovely book, Le Guin's world building and story telling are a step above. Full of interesting and challenging ideas, her stories shine a spotlight on the flaws of modern western society by showcasing an alternative society. "To a Karhider reading our canons, the crime of Judas lies not in his betrayal of Christ but in the act that, sealing despair, denies the chance of forgiveness, change, life: his suicide."
  11. Six Easy Pieces - Richard Feynman - 9/1/2026 It's hard to really delve into physics with a book this small but I think it's a good high level introduction to the concepts and how they were discovered. I particularly enjoyed Feynman's style of iterating on the different experiments, I can see why he was an amazing teacher.
  12. Solaris - Stanislaw Lem - 9/15/2026 Interesting concept of being unable to communicate with other intelligent life. I liked this book, the writing is good. "I hoped for nothing. And yet I lived in expectation. Since she had gone, that was all that remained. I did not know what achievements, what mockery, even what tortures still awaited me. I knew nothing, and I persisted in the faith that the time of cruel miracles was not past."
  13. The Things They Carried - Tim O'Brien - 10/6/2026 "For all the ambiguities of Vietnam, all the mysteries and unknowns, there was at least the single abiding certainty that they would never be at a loss for things to carry." "Before they die, though, one of the dead guys says "The fuck you do that for?" and the jumper says, "Story of my life, man" and the other guy starts to smile but he's dead."
